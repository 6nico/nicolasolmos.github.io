NVIDIA Semiconductor Supply Chain Risk & Optimization

Oct 2026 – Present

Built an Excel-based supply chain model to forecast semiconductor demand and evaluate supplier risks across different scenarios. The project simulates production delays and inventory strategies to measure stockout risk and operational performance.

Problem

Semiconductor supply chains face long lead times (14-20 weeks), geopolitical risks, and volatile demand. Companies need to balance inventory costs against stockout risk while diversifying suppliers.

Approach

Demand Forecasting: Created three scenarios (Base Case, High Growth +25%, Supply Constrained -15%) projecting quarterly demand through Q2 2028. Used VLOOKUP and conditional formatting to track market share trends.

Supplier Analysis: Evaluated five suppliers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, GlobalFoundries, SMIC) on lead time, capacity, reliability score, geopolitical risk, and cost per unit. Color-coded risk levels to visualize exposure.

Inventory Simulation: Simulated 12 weeks of inventory management using three reorder strategies: Conservative (40% threshold), Aggressive (25%), and Just-in-Time (15%). Tracked weekly demand, inventory levels, and stockout incidents.

Results

The Aggressive strategy (25% reorder threshold) proved optimal, balancing inventory costs with stockout prevention. JIT strategy created 15,200+ units of stockout risk over 12 weeks. Analysis showed 68% demand growth from Q1 2027 to Q2 2028, with TSMC and Samsung accounting for 52% of capacity but carrying high geopolitical risk.

Recommendations

Diversify to North American suppliers despite 12% higher costs. Maintain Aggressive reorder strategy. Monitor lead time changes and adjust safety stock accordingly.
